## Before You Start — The Golden Rules

1. **Listen first.** Before transmitting on any frequency, listen for at least 30 seconds. On a calling frequency, ask "Is this frequency in use?" before calling CQ.

2. **Keep it legal.** Always give your callsign at the start and end of a contact, and at least every 15 minutes during a long QSO.

3. **Be brief on calling frequencies.** Make contact, exchange callsigns, then move to a clear frequency. Don't have your whole conversation on the calling frequency.

4. **Speak clearly and steadily.** No need to shout or rush. Use the NATO phonetic alphabet for your callsign: "Mike Seven Alpha Bravo Charlie" not "M-7-A-B-C."

5. **Don't panic.** Everyone was new once. If you make a mistake, correct yourself and carry on. The amateur radio community is welcoming to new operators.

## Script 1: VHF/UHF FM Simplex

**When to use:** Making a contact on 2m (145.500 MHz) or 70cm (433.500 MHz) FM calling frequencies.

**How it works:** You call on the calling frequency, make contact, then both stations move to a clear channel to have your conversation. Never hold a long QSO on the calling frequency.

### Step 1 — Call CQ on the calling frequency

> Tune to **145.500 MHz** (2m) or **433.500 MHz** (70cm).
> Listen for 30 seconds to check it's clear.

**Say:**

> "CQ CQ CQ, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**, **YOUR CALLSIGN**, **YOUR CALLSIGN**. Calling CQ and standing by."

*Repeat two or three times with a short pause between each call. If no one responds, wait a minute and try again.*

### Step 2 — Someone responds

> A station comes back to your call.

**They say:**

> "YOUR CALLSIGN, this is **THEIR CALLSIGN**. Good evening, receiving you well."

### Step 3 — Acknowledge and find a clear frequency

**Say:**

> "**THEIR CALLSIGN**, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**. Good evening, thanks for coming back to me. Please stand by while I find a clear frequency."

*Tune up or down from the calling frequency to find an unused simplex channel. Listen for a few seconds to confirm it's clear.*

### Step 4 — Check the new frequency is clear

**On the new frequency, say:**

> "This is **YOUR CALLSIGN** — is this frequency in use?"

*Wait 10 seconds. If no one responds, it's clear. If someone says "Yes" or "QRL", try another channel.*

### Step 5 — Call the other station to the new frequency

**Go back to 145.500 MHz and say:**

> "**THEIR CALLSIGN**, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**. Please QSY to **[new frequency]**."

*Both stations change to the new frequency.*

### Step 6 — Start the conversation

**On the new frequency, say:**

> "**THEIR CALLSIGN**, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**. Are you receiving? Over."

**When they confirm, say:**

> "**THEIR CALLSIGN**, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**. My name is **[your name]** and my QTH is **[your location]**. Your signal report is **[RS report, e.g., 5 and 9]**. How do you copy? **THEIR CALLSIGN**, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**. Over."

### Step 7 — Close the contact

**When you're ready to finish, say:**

> "**THEIR CALLSIGN**, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**. Thanks for the contact today. I hope to speak with you again soon. 73 and best wishes. **THEIR CALLSIGN**, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**. Out."

> **"Over" vs "Out":** "Over" means "I've finished speaking, go ahead" — you expect a reply. "Out" means "I've finished, conversation is ending" — no reply expected. Never say "over and out" — it contradicts itself.

## Script 2: VHF/UHF FM Repeater

**When to use:** Making a contact through a local repeater. Repeaters extend your range by receiving on one frequency and retransmitting on another.

**How it works:** You don't need to call CQ on a repeater — just put your callsign out and see if anyone responds. Repeater contacts tend to be more casual than simplex.

### Step 1 — Call on the repeater

> Tune to the repeater output frequency. Set the correct CTCSS tone if required.

**Say:**

> "**YOUR CALLSIGN** listening through **[repeater callsign, e.g., GB3XX]**."

*Or simply:*

> "**YOUR CALLSIGN** listening."

*Wait for a response. If nobody comes back, wait a few minutes and try again.*

### Step 2 — Someone responds

**They say:**

> "**YOUR CALLSIGN**, this is **THEIR CALLSIGN**. Good afternoon."

### Step 3 — Have the conversation

**Say:**

> "**THEIR CALLSIGN**, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**. Good afternoon. My name is **[your name]**, QTH is **[your location]**. You're coming in **[signal report]** through the repeater. Over."

*Continue the conversation naturally. Remember to leave a gap between overs so other stations can break in if needed — this is called "leaving a gap" or "kerchunking the repeater."*

### Step 4 — Close the contact

**Say:**

> "**THEIR CALLSIGN**, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**. Thanks for the chat. 73 from **YOUR CALLSIGN**. Out."

> **Repeater etiquette:** Keep transmissions short — others may be waiting to use the repeater. Leave a 2-second gap between overs. If you hear a station calling "break" or "breaker", pause and let them in. Some repeaters have a timeout (typically 2–3 minutes) — if you talk too long, the repeater will drop your signal.

## Script 3: HF SSB (Phone)

**When to use:** Making contacts on the HF bands (80m, 40m, 20m, etc.) using single sideband voice. HF contacts are typically with more distant stations and follow a more structured format.

**Remember:** Below 10 MHz use **LSB**. Above 10 MHz use **USB**.

### Calling CQ

> Find a clear frequency. Listen for 30 seconds. Ask "Is this frequency in use?"

**Say:**

> "CQ CQ CQ, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**, **YOUR CALLSIGN**, **YOUR CALLSIGN**.
> **[Phonetics: e.g., Mike Seven Alpha Bravo Charlie]**.
> Calling CQ and standing by."

*Repeat two or three times. On HF, stations may be far away and signals can fade, so clear phonetics are essential.*

### When a station responds

**They say:**

> "YOUR CALLSIGN, this is **THEIR CALLSIGN**."

### Your first over

**Say:**

> "**THEIR CALLSIGN**, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**. Good afternoon and thanks for calling.
> My name is **[your name]**, name is **[name]**.
> My QTH is **[your town/city, country]**.
> Your signal report is **[RS, e.g., five nine]**.
> The rig here is **[your radio, e.g., IC-7610]** running **[power, e.g., 25 watts]** into a **[antenna, e.g., dipole at 10 metres]**.
> How do you copy? **THEIR CALLSIGN**, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**. Over."

> **What's in a typical HF exchange:** Name, location (QTH), signal report, rig and antenna, and power. This is the standard format — you'll hear it repeated across the bands. After the first exchange, the conversation can go anywhere: weather, equipment, conditions, other hobbies.

### Closing

**Say:**

> "**THEIR CALLSIGN**, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**. Thanks for the QSO, **[their name]**.
> I hope to catch you on the bands again. 73 and best wishes from **[your location]**.
> **THEIR CALLSIGN**, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**. Out."

## Script 4: Responding to a CQ Call

**When to use:** When you hear another station calling CQ and you want to respond. This is often easier than calling CQ yourself — someone else has done the hard work of finding a frequency and calling.

### You hear a CQ call

> "CQ CQ CQ, this is THEIR CALLSIGN..."

### Respond

**Wait until they finish calling, then say:**

> "**THEIR CALLSIGN**, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**, **[phonetics]**. Over."

*That's it — short and clear. Don't give your callsign more than twice. If they don't hear you, wait for their next CQ call and try again. If conditions are poor, you can add your location:*

> "**THEIR CALLSIGN**, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**, **[phonetics]**, in **[your location]**. Over."

## Script 5: CW (Morse Code)

**When to use:** Making a CW contact. CW QSOs follow a very standard format using Q-codes and abbreviations. Even at slow speeds, the structure makes contacts predictable and easy to follow.

> **Speed tip:** If you're learning, call on the QRS (slow CW) centres of activity, or add "QRS" to your CQ call. Most operators will happily slow down for you.

### Calling CQ

> `CQ CQ CQ DE YOUR CALLSIGN YOUR CALLSIGN YOUR CALLSIGN K`

*DE = "from". K = "go ahead."*

### When a station responds

> `YOUR CALLSIGN DE THEIR CALLSIGN THEIR CALLSIGN K`

### Standard CW exchange

> `THEIR CALLSIGN DE YOUR CALLSIGN`
> `GM/GA/GE` *(good morning/afternoon/evening)*
> `UR RST 579 579`
> `NAME IS [your name] [your name]`
> `QTH IS [your location]`
> `HW CPY? THEIR CALLSIGN DE YOUR CALLSIGN KN`

*KN = "go ahead, only the named station."*

### Closing

> `THEIR CALLSIGN DE YOUR CALLSIGN`
> `TU FER QSO [their name]`
> `HPE CUAGN 73 73`
> `THEIR CALLSIGN DE YOUR CALLSIGN SK`

*TU = thank you. FER = for. HPE CUAGN = hope to see you again. SK = end of contact.*

### Common CW Abbreviations

| Abbreviation | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| GM / GA / GE | Good morning / afternoon / evening |
| UR | Your / You're |
| RST | Signal report (Readability, Strength, Tone) |
| NAME | Name |
| QTH | Location |
| RIG | Equipment |
| ANT | Antenna |
| WX | Weather |
| TU | Thank you |
| FER | For |
| HPE | Hope |
| CUAGN | See you again |
| FB | Fine business (great!) |
| HW CPY? | How do you copy? |
| PSE | Please |
| AGN | Again |
| BK | Break |
| R | Roger / received |
| 73 | Best wishes |
| 88 | Love and kisses |

## Script 6: Digital Voice (D-STAR / DMR / Fusion)

**When to use:** Making contacts via digital voice modes through internet-linked repeaters or hotspots. Digital voice contacts can connect you worldwide via reflectors and talkgroups.

### D-STAR

**Step 1 — Select a reflector or gateway**

> Program your radio with the correct module (e.g., REF001C, XLX456A). Your callsign must be registered with the D-STAR gateway system.

**Step 2 — Call**

> "Calling on **[reflector name]**. This is **YOUR CALLSIGN**, **[phonetics]**. Is anyone around? Over."

**Step 3 — Standard exchange**

> Same as FM simplex — name, location, signal report, rig. Mention you're using D-STAR and which hotspot/repeater you're going through.

### DMR

> DMR uses **talkgroups** rather than reflectors. Select the appropriate talkgroup (e.g., TG235 for UK, TG91 for worldwide). Key up briefly to activate the talkgroup on the repeater/hotspot, wait 2 seconds, then call.

### Key differences from analogue

- **Wait for the "kerchunk" to clear** — digital systems have a brief setup delay. Wait 2 seconds after keying up before speaking.
- **No need to QSY** — talkgroups and reflectors are separate channels; you won't block other users unless you're on a busy talkgroup.
- **Registration required** — D-STAR, DMR, and Fusion all require your callsign to be registered before you can transmit.

## Script 7: Nets and Scheduled Groups

**When to use:** Joining an organised net — a scheduled on-air meeting where a net controller manages the conversation.

### Checking in to a net

> The net controller will call for check-ins:
> "This is **[net controller callsign]**, net control for the **[net name]**. Any stations wishing to check in?"

**Say:**

> "**YOUR CALLSIGN**."

*Just your callsign — keep it brief. The net controller will acknowledge you and add you to the list. Wait to be called before transmitting again.*

### When called by net control

**Say:**

> "**[Net controller callsign]**, this is **YOUR CALLSIGN**. **[Your traffic/comment]**. Back to net control."

> **Net etiquette:** Follow the net controller's instructions. Don't transmit unless called. If you need to leave early, ask to be excused. Nets are a great way to practise — you know someone will be there at the scheduled time.

## Signal Report Guide

### FM Signal Reports (RS — Readability and Strength)

On FM, tone is always 9 (clean), so we only give R and S:

| Readability (R) | Meaning |
|-----------------|---------|
| 1 | Unreadable |
| 2 | Barely readable, occasional words |
| 3 | Readable with considerable difficulty |
| 4 | Readable with practically no difficulty |
| 5 | Perfectly readable |

| Strength (S) | Meaning |
|--------------|---------|
| 1 | Barely perceptible |
| 3 | Weak |
| 5 | Fairly good |
| 7 | Moderately strong |
| 9 | Very strong |

*Common reports: "5 and 9" (perfect), "5 and 7" (good), "3 and 5" (readable but not strong).*

> **Be honest.** Giving everyone "5 and 9" regardless of actual signal quality is a common habit — but honest reports are more useful. If you can barely hear them, say so. They might adjust their antenna or power.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | What to do instead |
|---------|---------------|-------------------|
| "Over and out" | Contradicts itself — "over" means "your turn", "out" means "finished" | Use "over" during the QSO, "out" at the end |
| "CQ CQ, this is..." (only twice) | Too short — stations may miss it | Call CQ three times, give your callsign three times |
| Talking on the calling frequency | Blocks others from making contact | Move to a clear channel after initial contact |
| Not giving your callsign | Legal requirement | Give callsign at start, end, and every 15 minutes |
| Speaking too fast | Especially on HF with fading signals | Speak clearly at a moderate pace |
| "Breaker breaker" | CB radio language, not amateur radio | Use "break" if you need to interrupt |
| "73s" | 73 already means "best wisheS" (plural) | Just say "73" |
| Not listening first | You might transmit over an existing QSO | Always listen before transmitting |
