How Reciprocal Mixing Works
  • The local oscillator (LO) is not a pure tone — its phase noise creates a noise skirt around f_LO
  • A strong adjacent-channel signal at f_LO ± Δf mixes with the LO noise skirt offset by −Δf
  • The mixing product lands directly in the IF passband, raising the noise floor by the interferer's power minus the phase-noise density at that offset
  • The noise rise is: ΔNF ≈ P_int + PN(Δf) + 10·log10(BW) relative to thermal noise floor
  • A better LO (lower phase noise) or a narrower roofing filter before the mixer reduces the effect
Key Formulae
Phase noise slope: L(Δf) ≈ L(1kHz) − 20·log10(Δf/1kHz) [dBc/Hz]
Noise rise in IF: ΔNF = P_int + L(Δf) + 10·log10(BW) [dBm]
IF noise ≥ thermal when: ΔNF > −174 dBm/Hz + NF_rx + 10·log10(BW)
Reciprocal mixing spurs: f_IF ± Δf from each RF interferer offset ±Δf from channel
