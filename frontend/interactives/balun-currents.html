No balun: The coax carries three currents. The inner conductor feeds one dipole arm (cyan). The inside of the braid returns current through the other arm (purple) — these two are differential and balanced. But a third current flows on the outside of the braid (red) — the common-mode current. This turns the coax itself into a radiator, distorting the pattern and causing RFI at the shack.

With a choke balun: The ferrite core presents a very high impedance (hundreds of Ω) to common-mode current while leaving the differential signal unaffected. The outside-braid current is choked off, the dipole sees a balanced feed, and the pattern is symmetric.
