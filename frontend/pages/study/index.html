UK licence privileges at a glance

The three-tier ladder as it stands under the post-1-September-2024 Ofcom framework. Refer back here for a side-by-side comparison of what each tier can do.

Privilege Foundation Intermediate Full
Max transmit power 25 W PEP most bands (10 W on some restricted bands) 100 W PEP most bands (50 W on some; 1 W on 135.7 kHz; 32 W on upper 1.8 MHz) 1 000 W PEP (30 dBW) on most primary HF / VHF / UHF bands; specialist exceptions (60 m / 5 MHz: 15 W EIRP; 472 kHz: 1 W EIRP; 135.7 kHz: 1 W EIRP)
Callsign format M7xxx or ME7xxx 2E0xxx / 2E1xxx (traditional) or M8xxx / M9xxx (modernised blocks) M0xxx, G0xxx (and historic prefixes)
Build your own transmitter? Commercial gear OR kits conforming to Ofcom IR 2028 OR self-build limited to ≤ 2 W on 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands only. Cannot scratch-build custom transmitters. Full freedom to design, build, modify and operate home-brew transmitters from scratch. No kit-only limitation. Full freedom to design, build, alter and test any transmitting or receiving hardware.
Operating location UK only. Maritime mobile (/MM) permitted in UK territorial waters. No foreign operation without a separate local licence. UK only. Expanded maritime mobile privileges. No CEPT reciprocal recognition abroad. UK plus full CEPT reciprocal — operate in participating CEPT countries without applying for a local licence.
Frequency bands Most standard VHF / UHF / HF amateur bands, plus 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz. Denied specialist narrow bands (5 MHz / 60 m, 472 kHz / 630 m). Same broad access as Foundation, with full power allowance across those bands. All amateur bands including 5 MHz (60 m) and 472 kHz (630 m). Access to microwave bands up to 3 THz under NoV.
Notice of Variation (NoV) eligibility Not eligible. Not eligible. Eligible to apply for NoVs to operate repeaters, internet gateways, beacons, or high-microwave bands.
Airborne / aeronautical mobile (/AM) 500 mW EIRP across all three tiers, on primary amateur bands only. Airborne limit is set by radio regulation, not by licence tier.

2024 framework note. The figures above reflect the post-1-September-2024 Ofcom licence framework. If you see older study materials quoting Foundation 10 W, Intermediate 50 W or Full 400 W, those are the pre-2024 limits that were increased under the licensing review. Cross-reference against the current Ofcom Amateur Radio Licence document or the RSGB 3rd Edition Intermediate Manual if in doubt.
