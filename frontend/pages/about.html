About RF-Hub

What is RF-Hub?

An interactive amateur radio study companion — built by a dyslexic learner, for every kind of learner.

What RF-Hub is

RF-Hub is a free interactive study companion for the UK amateur radio licence exams — Foundation, Intermediate, and Full (Direct-to-Full). It exists alongside the official RSGB manuals, not instead of them.

Every study section maps to specific RSGB syllabus items. Self-check questions let you test recall in the browser. Mock exams let you practise under timed conditions before the real thing. The goal is to make the content easier to absorb, especially for learners who struggle with dense printed text.

The story

RF-Hub was built by Anthony, M0ANT — a dyslexic learner who found traditional text-heavy study manuals hard to work with. When studying for his own licence exams, he built the interactive notes and self-checks he wished had existed.

That material has since grown into a full study site. The design choices — clear section headings, short paragraphs, generous whitespace, interactive reveals — aren't just aesthetic. They're the specific things that made the content stick for a learner whose brain works differently from a conventional text reader.

It's now shared openly with the wider amateur radio community, in the spirit of the hobby itself: self-training, experimentation, and helping others learn.

Relationship with the RSGB

Important: This site is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Radio Society of Great Britain. RSGB publications are the authoritative source for licence preparation. RF-Hub aims to be a companion to those materials, not a replacement.

For definitive answers, always refer to:

  • The current Ofcom Amateur Radio Licence document — the authoritative regulatory source.
  • The RSGB Intermediate Licence Manual (3rd Edition, 2024), Foundation Licence Manual, or Full Licence Manual — as appropriate for your level of study.

RF-Hub study notes are checked against those sources, but exam question wording, syllabus weighting, and licence conditions can change. If you spot a discrepancy, the official source wins — and we'd like to know about it (see Feedback below).

How the content is built

Every study section traces back to specific RSGB syllabus items. Callouts are tagged with their syllabus reference (e.g. 1G1, D2F-M1-Q11) so you can cross-reference directly against the manual. Content is regularly cross-checked against the current RSGB manuals, Ofcom Amateur Radio Licence schedule, and examination authority guidance.

When the licensing framework changes — as it did on 1 September 2024 when Ofcom updated the power limits — we update the site content to reflect the new framework and explicitly flag what changed for learners who may have seen the old figures in earlier study materials.

Accessibility

Dyslexic-friendly design is a first-class concern, not an afterthought:

  • Clear heading hierarchy — you should always know where you are in a section.
  • Short paragraphs and generous whitespace — no walls of text.
  • Monospace labels and code — clearly distinguished from body prose.
  • Interactive reveals — self-check answers are hidden until you ask for them, reducing cognitive load during first reading.
  • Keyboard-navigable — all interactive elements are reachable without a mouse.
  • Semantic HTML landmarks — <nav>, <main>, <section>, <footer> throughout. Working toward WCAG 2.1 AA.

Feedback & contact

Accuracy is a first-class concern. If you find an error — a wrong figure, a stale licence condition, a calculation that doesn't add up — please report it. Content corrections are genuinely welcome and acted on promptly.
